## What it is about

An ending that never happened, and four witches who are four sizes of answer to it: the moment, nature, chaos and infinity. One evening, told as a show in four acts that ends in a curtain call.

## The premise (stand-ins until Chris says what wisps and wraiths are)

- Long ago the airship beacon on the crag was left untended. Its fire did not die. It went cold and hungry, and it has been calling every flame within reach ever since.
- A wisp is a flame that answered the call and went cold on the way. Sad, not wicked, but it puts out what it touches.
- A Shadow Wraith is a wisp that has been cold so long it has forgotten it was a flame. It serves the call and takes light for the tower.
- The great wraith is the old fire itself, wearing every light it has pulled in.
- Tonight the Moon Faire, the brightest thing this moor will see in a lifetime, has come within reach.

## Four acts, in the order the evening darkens

1. **The Ranger, the moment. The fair at dusk.** She sees it happen: every flame leaning with no wind, wisps passing through without attacking, all going east, the captain's twelve lamps tearing off their hooks. She says what this night is: the only night this fair will ever stand here.
2. **Io, nature. The moor.** After the first win she holds a beaten wisp until it is warm, and it drifts home toward the fair. Healing here is giving a thing back to its nature. She finds the limit too: the fire in the tower cannot be healed, because nothing is wrong with it. It is a fire nobody minds.
3. **The Fox, chaos. The standing stones, then the mooring field.** She sees the call as thread, one from every flame, pulled tight toward the crag. She cuts one. When the twelfth lamp is home she sews each flame down with cold blue thread and rings her bell, which is why the captain can fly.
4. **The Jester, infinity (life and death). The crag.** The fire's hunger has no end, a small sad copy of what she is. On the stair she is the only one laughing. In the lamp room she calls the hunger onto herself, and her Curtain Call gives the fire the ending it never had.

- **The count:** twelve lamps, as the paintings show (3 at the Last Lantern, 4 at the stones, 5 at the pool). Each lies on the moor with cold wisps clinging to its warmth; winning lets the wisps go and brings the lamp back.
- **Why they go on:** twelve lamps home changes nothing at the tower. The call pulls again tomorrow, and a fair that follows the moon cannot outrun it.
- **The ending:** the lights the fire took fall as star dust and go home every way at once. One goes back to the dead post at the Last Lantern. One small ordinary flame is left in the beacon. Last line, the Ranger: "Thirteen lights. I counted."

## Why this one

- The Jester's power is not light. It is the night sky, and every lamp gutters when she casts (`claude/jester-game-model.md`). A fire that hoards light is the one story where that power is the answer and not only the biggest hit.
- Her taunt becomes story: she draws the call onto herself.
- The four scales Chris gave the witches (the moment, nature, chaos, infinity) become the four acts.
- No painting changes. It joins Opus's options A (twelve lamps) and C (the cold hearth), with the Fox's stitching from D as her moment.
