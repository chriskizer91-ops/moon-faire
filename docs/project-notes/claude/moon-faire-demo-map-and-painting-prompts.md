## Does it have to be lamps? Options from Opus 5.5 (high effort, shown the paintings)

No. Four options; none needs a painting redone. They are options, not decisions.

- **A. Keep the lamps, give them a reason.** The great wraith has hoarded stolen light for years; the twelve lamps were the newest. When she falls the light streams home; the beacon stays dark. Nothing to change. Weak point: once the lamps are home, the climb is an errand.
- **B. The beacon's broken crystal heart.** The beacon ran on solar crystal; the heart cracked and wisps carry its pieces. The four gather them and set the heart back in its cradle; the beacon shines again. Shows crystal power and why beacons matter. Weak point: "collect the pieces" is familiar, and it makes the demo explain crystal power.
- **C. The cold hearth calls every flame** (Opus's pick). The dead beacon's fire went cold but not out; the great wraith is that fire, calling every flame for miles. Wisps are flames that answered and went cold. The fair, the brightest light here in a lifetime, is within reach tonight. Beating a wisp sets its light free. At the end the old fire gets its hearth back and the beacon burns warm. Weak point: the count is less solid in the hand, and fighting wisps to free them needs one clear line.
- **D. The Fox sews the night shut.** Something has cut the night open; wisps slip through. The Fox sews it shut with each beaten wisp's light as thread; the great wraith holds the cut open at the crag. A story about one of the four. Weak point: it raises "what is on the other side", which the demo can't answer.

What could be joined (Opus): A's twelve lamps as the count, with C's reason underneath (the lamps were pulled, not stolen). B's crystal heart could be what lies cold in the cradle in C. The Fox's stitch could be a small moment at the end of any.

Opus's questions for Chris:
1. Are the wisps villains, or sad lights that can be set free?
2. At the end, should the beacon burn, or stay dark as painted?
3. Should the demo show crystal power at work, or keep it in the background?
4. Should the story be the captain's problem, or something that reaches the four themselves?

Each option leans on something not yet settled: what wraiths and wisps are in this world. That is Chris's to say.
