# Lore discussion notes

Chris's words and decisions, gathered while the lore is talked through. Nothing here is final yet. Started November 12, 2026, with more rounds added the same day and on November 13. Lines marked "Claude's note" are suggestions, not Chris's words.

## The world

- **Moon Faire is its own game:** an alternate universe, or the same universe, "from the same place" as Chris's other games (Envoi, Witch Way, the Aethermoor games).
- **November 13: the story takes place on a different continent of Thareia,** not Aethermoor. Chris: everything he has made so far is from the continent of Aethermoor, on the world Thareia, whose moon is Auros. First he said the four "could be from a different continent" on that planet; later the same morning: "I think this story takes place on a different continent." (On November 12 it was only "may not be set in Aethermoor at all, for now.")
  - Claude's note: this fits Chris's October decision that as many stories as possible are set on Thareia, on its moon, or on one of the other planets in his lore. The older docs only ever called Aethermoor Thareia's *known* realm, so nothing written so far rules out other continents.
- **A different time period** (November 13). Chris: "this has to be a different time period because the moon has to be above where they are. So this is a different number of years" from his other games.
- **Moon phases are not part of their magic** (November 13). Chris: "them having moon phases as part of their magic messes up the situation." This drops the older moon rule (each witch drawing on one phase, the less moon the more magic), and the idea from earlier the same morning that the new continent sits under a new moon, the Jester's phase.
- **The Moon Faire is a normal fair** (November 13). The four are part of it: they "just happen to be like traveling with the Moon Faire". Chris: "they're called their group" (wording unclear; a name for the four's group may be meant).
  - **It is called the Moon Faire because it follows the moon around the planet.**
  - It is the most melting-pot community there is: of cultures, identities and knowledge.
- **Auros goes once round Thareia every 500 years,** "because it is like almost tidally locked with Thareia" (November 13). It hangs almost still in the sky and drifts slowly round the world, and the fair follows it.
  - Claude's note, what follows from it (not confirmed by Chris):
    - It is real physics: Pluto and its moon Charon are fully locked to each other, so Charon never moves in Pluto's sky.
    - Any one place sees the moon for about 250 years, then not at all for the next 250. For most places the fair passing through is a once-in-a-lifetime event.
    - On a planet Earth's size, the fair moves about 80 km (50 miles) a year to stay under it.
    - If the new continent is on the far side of the planet from Aethermoor, this story is about 250 years before or after the other games.
    - Under the moon it looks new at noon and full at midnight, every day, so the fair always has a full moon overhead at night.
- **A Dark Ages fantasy.** It has what looks like steam power, "but it's not actually steam power. It's like the solar crystal power."
  - Claude's note: Aethermoor's airships already fly on amber sunstone lift in the earlier drafts.
- **Space travel.** You can go into space and travel to the moon. Space is not a vacuum: "it actually is this like magical thing."
  - **The moon is the wealthy place:** "financial", "the wealthy shipgoers".
  - **Other planets are nearby,** reached on "real fancy space airship things that fly through space".
  - None of that needs to be in Moon Faire, apart from an airship.
- **There will be an airship in this game.** Chris: "I keep putting airships in games. I like them a lot." Airship travel is a big part of this world.
- Chris: "we have to think about those things."
- It will reuse assets from those games, because of how they look and how much content was made for them. It may borrow their lore too; that is not settled.
- Chris's goal is to keep building all of these games, in the same universe, as newer models come out. Putting them all together is not planned soon.
- Details can differ from game to game: "multiverse type stuff". What Io summons, for example, can change between games.
- They all come from the same studio: Chris and Claude.

## Combat

- **November 13: rework the combat from the fundamentals.** Chris, after reading the first balance draft: keep none of its HP levels, and none of the way the spells work or block. Keep only the animations. The aim is for combat to make more sense, be more playable and be more epic.
- **The agreed system is in `combat-balance.md`** (first version, November 13): every rule, move and number, how the fights play, and the open questions. It was worked out with two consultant agents over three rounds, using Envoi's balance method. It runs in the blob battle, https://claude.ai/artifact/EmDURhEiinMwqyfi9DasDx.
- **Chris, after playing it in different conditions (November 13): he likes the balance.**
  - **Encounters at the party's level are balanced as they are.**
  - **Easier or harder comes from simple, obvious things in the game, without changing the mechanics:** for example a find that raises the party's healing by about 5%: a genuine help to winning, measurable and obvious, that only slightly increases the party's ability to survive. Chris: there should be simple in-game ways to make it more challenging or easier, very simply, without changing the mechanics of the balance.
  - **Zones, as in Envoi:** ordinary foes can be up to four whole levels above the party. Those are always the weak foes, never bosses.
  - **A single boss:** the party has to be within a level of it.
  - **The big boss at the end of an area:** the party has to be at the same level as that boss.
  - **The numbers keep scaling,** 20% a level for both sides, so the game can keep climbing: the Jester can reach a power level of 100,000 and it still works. Chris: "I was raised on anime and just making the numbers bigger is fun."
- **Turn-based, "like chess"** (November 13): nobody should be penalized for not clicking a button fast enough. The blob battle already waits for every move. Whether to keep each fighter's speed on the turn bar or switch to rounds is open (`combat-balance.md`).
- Claude's note: the Jester's game rules in the next section are the older ideas from November 12. Where they differ from `combat-balance.md`, the combat doc is the current version. In particular, Fate now grows by a set amount per blow on her, and Sealed Fate ends a sealed foe outright with a splash, rather than charging at 75% of her HP and taking 75% of a foe's HP.

## The Jester witch

- The most powerful of the four.
- **In the story she shows up later on.** The player builds the party first. Chris: that is why her power is fine for the game's balance.
- **Not the frailest. Her HP is the highest.** She shrugs off damage: she can be battered and bruised and still fight at full power.
- Her normal attack is not that strong.
- **She uses her wit and cunning to catch foes' attention and take the blows.** A single-target attack goes to her. An attack that hits everyone still hits everyone.
- **She is the tank** (later on November 12). She takes the damage and shields the rest of the party from the biggest foe, or from all the foes, by taunting them. Chris expects her to be the reason the party beats most foes and wins most battles.
- **Her taunts are moves that also do something:**
  - **Attack taunt:** the not-HD model's Gravity mixed with the HD model's twirl, dance and other physical moves.
  - **Heal taunt:** Against All Odds. It heals only herself, and it is not as good as a heal from someone else.
  - She should have even more dance moves, and act like a jester and laugh as she does them.
  - While she attack-taunts, Io heals her, so she can take even more powerful hits.
- **Her big spell** removes a large share of a foe's total HP (Chris suggested 75%). The share is of the foe's maximum HP, not the HP it has left.
  - **Its animation:** Sealed Fate is how it begins. **Curtain Call, the closing, plays only when the cast brings every foe to zero:** an actual curtain call, the end of the show. Chris: that also gives room for levelling her up.
  - **It charges by damage, not by a count of hits** (later on November 12; this replaces the earlier rule of three hits, then four, and so on).
    - Every blow aimed at her charges it, "whether or not she's taunted them".
    - It is full when the blows add up to 75% of her total HP.
    - There is still no turn countdown once it is charged.
  - **She does not take all of that damage.** She might take half, or her heal taunt might heal her for a lot, or Io might heal her: "that's all like balancing stuff". Either way she is "absorbing the enemies' hits, charging up a single massive hit".
  - **How the charge looks:** "an aura of dust that gets thicker and thicker until she uses it". Very simple, single pixels, cheap to draw. When she casts, it "manifests out of the air as those rings", in the two-part cast (Sealed Fate into Curtain Call).
    - Chris was undecided about rings turning round her while it charges. He likes that it is not obvious something is building.
    - Built on the HD model the same day: `claude/jester-3d-pass20-charge-as-dust.md`.
  - **The lore's spells need to be more dramatic,** with a "battle ending guaranteed" feeling. Add effects and graphics to them; do not change them. Chris likes the moves and their timing as built in pass 19.
  - Ideas Chris weighed in the same talk and has not settled:
    - Damage as a ratio of her own HP, which will always be high.
    - Returning exactly what she absorbed: "absorbing their hits and then throwing it back".
    - Raw, unlimited power, since she is infinity. "Doom is like the duality of life and death", and her spell "just ends the foe she casts it on".
  - Chris: if she is getting healed while she takes the hits, "that's the team's method".
  - Cast early, it leaves foes so weak that her weak attack can finish them, or her companions can, very easily.
  - **It ends fights.** Chris's picture of the ideal moment:
    - A big boss fight is balanced so the party seems to be losing. Most of them are below half HP. After six swings the boss still has about two-thirds of its health.
    - Then her spell lands, and the fight is over at once.
  - She can still cast it if everyone else falls. Her HP is high enough to take the hits.
  - Claude's note: at 75% of maximum HP, the early cast and the fight-ending cast are one spell. Cast at the start, it leaves the foe on 25%. Cast once the party has done 25% (or more), it ends the fight.
  - Claude's note: returning the same points she absorbed would not end a fight, because a foe's HP is usually many times her own. Claude suggested the same share of a life instead: she absorbs 75% of her HP, and the foe loses 75% of its HP. Chris answered that the charge counts damage. He has not said in words whether the foe's loss stays at 75% of its maximum HP, so the line above still stands.
  - Claude's note: the party's plan grows out of her.
    - Her taunts draw the hits that charge the spell.
    - Io's heals keep her standing while she takes them.
    - The Ranger's strong attacks finish whatever the spell leaves.
    - The Fox makes enemies hit themselves or leave the party alone. That means fewer hits on the Jester, so the player chooses between the Fox's control and letting the Jester soak up the hits.
- **How strong she is alone.** On her own, taking all the hits without any healing, she could probably beat more than one very powerful foe. Out in the open she could beat most things, unless they are many levels above her and come in numbers.
- **How the game's fights will push her:** fights of four or five creatures, counting the boss and several assistants that are also quite powerful, and several stages of fighting, so that she has to cast her big spell many times.
- **Her lesson:** learning to defend, to fight faster, and to do her job efficiently, so that her friends survive.
- Balance is still to be worked out. This is lore for now.

## The Fox

- The chaos-magic witch: an artist who creates reality and can use it.
- **A fabric artist** (November 12, while her 3D reference paintings were being planned): "the fabric of reality, scissors, and a needle and thread". Her scissors become daggers. Needles, and a bell. She sews reality.
- She is playful.
- **Her fox ears are costume ears** that her character is wearing. They are not real. She has no tail, apart from ghost tails in spells.
- **Her hair is one real, messy ponytail bun.** The front of her painting and the back views on her character sheet are both correct: the same hair from two sides.
- **Her legs are very wide patchwork trousers.**
- **Her magic's colour is cold blue foxfire.** Chris is open to an interpretation that makes it more vibrant and beautiful: "an artist who creates reality should have a varied magical power color".
- Several damage attacks, at the upper-middle tier.
- She can make an enemy attack itself. **This has to be something special.** If a ghost 3D copy of any character in the game can be made, that is the direction.
- She is the reason the party can get away from a fight, and she can make enemies not attack them.
  - **Getting away:** the party is able to flee, shown in some creative way that does not need a copy of each of them left standing while they run.
- Much of the rest of her magic is confusing the enemy, with illusion and chaos.
- **Her 3D demo can have more spells than the game will use,** as long as the ones in this lore are there. Chris asked for one image of each of the old move sheet's other seven moves and left their use to Claude. He expects her spells to need the most animation of the four.
- Chris to Claude, for the details he has not set: "You are the artist channeling her as the character."
- Claude's note: the spell list drawn up for her model is in `claude/fox-3d-reference-prompts.md`, with working names: Same Cloth (a ghost copy of the enemy, cut out with her shears and worked on threads, strikes the enemy), Slip Stitch (she cuts a slit in the air, the party goes through, and it is sewn shut behind them) and Crazy Quilt (confusion). None of it is confirmed.

## Io, the Herbal Witch (the one with the red hat)

- Her attacks are low tier.
- **Her heals are good, at the middle tier.** She can heal the whole party by some share, or heal one person a lot.
- **Her summons are one-time moves, at the upper-middle tier of damage,** not the top.
  - In Envoi her summons were the finisher, because she was the only one with power. In Moon Faire, the Jester is the finisher.
  - One idea from Chris: one damage move at the low end of the upper tier, and one summon at the upper-middle tier.
  - She summons deities, with a paladin-like energy.
  - What she summons can change between games, and will fit this game's story.
- She is presumably why the party has herbal healing and potion-type items in Chris's games.
- She first came from another of Chris's games, where she was named Io. He mentioned *Follow Me Down Witch Way*, an herb-gathering game, as the first game he made. (In the repos, *Envoi on the Longest Night* names her Io; Witch Way calls her "the witch".)

## The blue Ranger

- The weakest magic user, though not necessarily the weakest fighter.
- A princess with magical powers.
- Very good with a bow, a sword and knives. Her strength is her regular attack.
- Her magic is self-buffing: she uses it to strengthen her own attacks.
