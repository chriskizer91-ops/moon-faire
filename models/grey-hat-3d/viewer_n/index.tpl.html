New: his hat, belt, pouch, scroll and jewellery

  • You said he looks good as he is, and to give him his hat, his belt and pouch and scroll and jewellery.
  • The hat. Grey wool felt with a wide drooping brim, a dark band, and a slumped crown whose point falls to his left, as your paintings have it. His hair comes out from under it. Press "Hat" under the views. The point leans a little when his head moves.
  • Pearls at the base of his throat, inside the open collar, and the feather earring in his left ear only: a short chain, two small pearls and a black feather. It swings.
  • The belt. Black leather over the bottom of the corset, fastened in front by one large steel ring, with a buckle at his left and a second strap dropping to his right hip. Press "Belt".
  • On his right hip: the brown pouch with its flap and brass stud, the rolled scroll in its leather loop under it, and the skull and leaf charms. On his left hip: the dagger in its sheath.
  • Press "Walk". The pouch, scroll, charms and dagger hang from the belt and swing and settle as the Ranger's bags do. His thigh pushes them out as it comes forward, so they do not pass through his trousers.
  • Under Show, "Hat" and "Belt and jewellery" take them off and put them back.
  • Rough for now: the hat's folds are simpler than the painted ones. The pearls go under the collar at the sides instead of lying over it. The scroll is plain parchment with no writing. The ring is a little bigger than painted. The scroll hangs a few centimetres nearer his middle than painted, and the dagger hangs straight down where the painting slants it. In "Walk" and "Talk" his right hand passes close to the pouch.
  • I cannot watch him move from here, only look at stills. Tell me if the hanging things swing too much or too little.

Before that: he stands and walks like a man

  • He stands with his weight on his right leg and his arms by his sides. In "Walk" his heel lands first, the foot rolls and the toe pushes off. In "Talk" he gestures with both hands.
  • Rough: he walks on the spot, and the boots are still the old painted leather.

Before that: his hair is hair, over real ears

  • His hair is built from about 130 wavy locks over a thin cap, with real ears and glasses that rest on them. It moves when he walks or nods. "Old hair" puts the old shell back.
  • Rough: the top of his head looks combed flat, the hairline is a hard edge, and close up the strands look scratchy.

Before that: a real shirt that moves

  • You asked for paint on his lower arms and upper chest first, then the shirt on top. Both are done.
  • The doll is painted. His forearms have skin, shading and dark hair. His chest has collarbones, a little hair and soft shading, and so do his belly, back and upper arms. Press "Doll" to see it with the clothes off.
  • The shirt is its own piece of cloth now. Nothing of his body is printed on it. It has a body, two full sleeves gathered into a band below the elbow, lace cuffs in two layers, a ruffled front in three layers, a collar, and dark red lacing through eight small eyelets. In the open front you see his real chest behind the lacing.
  • It moves. The sleeves, cuffs, ruffles and the loose part of the body hang by their own weight, lag behind him and settle. Press "Walk" or "Talk" to see it. His arms are kept inside his sleeves.
  • The lace is cut from your close painting of the fabrics, with real holes in it. The linen is from the same painting.
  • The trousers sway a little when he walks: the baggy part trails his legs by up to a centimetre. The holes are untouched.
  • The corset is still the old painted piece, cut down to just the corset, sitting over the shirt.
  • Press "Old shirt" to put the old painted shirt back and compare.
  • Rough for now: the ruffled front is flatter and tidier than you painted it. There is a small step in the cloth at each top corner of the open front, by his neck. The sleeves are plainer than you painted them. The leather wrist cuffs are still the old painted piece and their top edge is uneven.
  • I cannot watch him move from here, only look at stills. If the shirt does something odd when he walks or talks, tell me.

The test renderer I use here draws it anyway, which is why I never saw it. It is fixed, and the build now stops if it ever happens again.

Where he is: first build

  • You said he looks pretty good and not to remake him from scratch, so everything else is as it was.
  • His head is now the head the witches wear (the Jester's face model, at a man's size, with a wider jaw and a lower crown), with his own face from C1 painted on it and his profile from C3 at the sides. Press "Blank doll" to see the shape with no paint.
  • His glasses are a real object.
  • His body is measured from B1 and B2 and wears those paintings, laid on from four sides. His sleeves take their sides from A3.
  • His hands are the full hand model the witches use, at a man's size, in two plain colours: glove leather and skin.
  • He stands 1.78 m in his boots. That is my number for a man of average height; nothing in the paintings fixes it.

The gold on his boots

  • Flecks sit on the leather of each boot and move with it. Each one glints in its own time.
  • Motes drift slowly round his feet while he stands.
  • The trail: a boot sheds dust whenever it moves, and the dust hangs where it was shed and fades. Here he walks on the spot, so the dust streams away behind him. In a game it will be left along his path.

Rough, or not built

  • His neck is one plain skin colour, with no painting on it.
  • His body is his clothes as one smooth skin, not a body with clothes on it. The chest ruffles, lace, corset straps, buckles, trouser holes and boot straps are paint, not shapes, and the paint is soft up close. Only the sleeve ruffles are real shapes.
  • Under his arms and between his legs the paint is drawn out, because no painting looks there.
  • His face does not blink or talk yet (C2 is not used yet). His hand paintings (D) and boot paintings (E) are not used yet either.
  • Not built: the hat, the belt and its ring, the pouch, the scroll, the charms, the dagger, the pearls and the feather earring. Rounds 1 and 2 of your paintings for all of these are saved.
  • The walk and the talk are first drafts borrowed from the Ranger's stride. Nothing bends as cloth. I cannot watch movement play here, so its timing needs your eye.
