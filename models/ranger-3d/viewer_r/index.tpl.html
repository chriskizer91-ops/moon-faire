Notes on this build

Changed in this pass

  • Her two throwing knives are built from your knife painting: each one piece of moonsteel, 20 cm long, with a ring holding a crescent moon, a handle bound in dark blue cord and a leaf-shaped blade.
  • They sit handles up in a black double sheath with a blue stud, at the small of her back just under her belts. The folded bow rides 2 cm higher than before to make room.
  • New move, "Knives". Both hands go behind her and take a knife each. She hops her feet wide with her chest to us, right hand up by her head and left hand low in front, as in your painting. She coils, throws the first with her right hand as her body turns toward screen right, and the second is already coming up: she throws it with her left as her body turns back. Each knife flies point first with a thin trail of light.
  • A choice I made: after the throw the knives come back to the sheath in a small glint of light, so the move can be played again. Say if you would rather they stayed gone.
  • New switch: "Knives", under "More views and switches".

Earlier: her sword

  • Her sword is built from your sword painting: a slim moonsteel blade, a crescent guard with its horns toward the blade, a dark blue grip bound with silver wire, and an oval blue gem in gold with a ring of gold beads for a pommel. 74 cm long.
  • It hangs in a black scabbard with a silver mouth and tip at the front of her left hip, tip down and back, as in your carry painting. The scabbard swings as she moves, and her thigh pushes it aside instead of passing through it.
  • New move, "Sword". Her left hand tips the scabbard, her right hand crosses and draws. She hops round to face screen right and lands on guard, right foot leading. The blade goes back over her shoulder, then comes over the top in one full cut with a long step, leaving a crescent of light. She holds the lunge, comes back to guard, turns to the front and puts the sword away.
  • Her feet are planted in this move: each foot is put on a spot on the ground and her legs are worked out from it, the same way as her arms.
  • New switch: "Sword", under "More views and switches".

Earlier: fingers, the bow, Moonsteel Draw

  • Her fingers curl. Her hands hang relaxed, close round what she holds, open when she talks and make a fist when she cheers.
  • Her bow is built from your bow paintings: moonsteel, a dark blue leather grip, a crescent holding a blue gem at each end of the grip, and open limbs of two rails with a vine and leaves between them. It is 96 cm from tip to tip.
  • Folded, each limb rolls up toward its tip like a fern, and the bow rides upright on the middle of her back, 37 cm long. It stays there while she walks, jogs, talks and cheers.
  • "Take aim" is now "Moonsteel Draw". She pulls the bow from her back with her right hand, brings it round her side, takes the grip in her left, and the limbs unroll. Sparks show where the string will be, then the string of light. She hooks it with three fingers, pushes the bow out and pulls back to the corner of her jaw, holds with her eyes narrowed, and lets go. The arrow of light flies toward screen right, her hand springs back with the fingers open, the bow dips, rolls up and goes back on her back.
  • Her arms are now worked out from where her hands need to be, so her left hand stays on the grip and her right on the string all the way through.
  • The string, the sparks and the arrow are light only. In the Flipbook look they stay soft and get no ink line.
  • New buttons: "Back" in the camera row, and a "Bow" switch under "More views and switches".

Earlier: sleeves, soft things, feet, the page

  • Sleeves, a different way altogether: the lace keeps its painted length and shape in every pose. Her forearms and hands are simply always drawn in front of the lace, so wherever they cross you see her whole arm with the lace behind it.
  • Sleeves, a different way: as soon as her elbow bends, the lace is pushed up her arm, shorter and closer, so its hem ends well above her elbow and her forearm has nothing to pass through. With her arm straight it hangs to her elbow as painted.
  • "Flipbook look" is a new switch beside "Half speed". It redraws her at her sprites' pixel size, in the 56 colours all four witches share, with an inked outline. It starts off.
  • Sleeves again: a folded elbow now gathers the lace small and close round her upper arm, short of her forearm, and the loop rides a little above her arm instead of lying on it.
  • Sleeves, done again: each is now a loop of cloth round her arm. It rests on the top of her arm and hangs below it, her forearm pushes it aside and gathers it toward her shoulder when her elbow folds, and her arm always stays inside it. It follows on a spring, so it sways.
  • Soft things: her lace sleeves, the two tails of her headband and her sixteen loose locks now hang and swing. Each lags when she moves and settles when she stops. A sleeve hangs further down the more her elbow folds, so in "Take aim" her forearm no longer passes through it.
  • Her shoulders: each arm now turns in her shoulder like a ball in a socket, so nothing pinches or steps where arm meets body when she raises it. Her armpit opens a little as it lifts. Her shape standing still is unchanged.
  • Her feet: the ankle now bends only as far as an ankle can, the foot hangs loose while it is in the air, and her stiff boots no longer fold at the toe.
  • Less stiff: each part of her body moves a beat behind the one below it, her arms hang loose, her head drifts a little.
  • The jog is lighter and more upright, with softer arms.
  • The short moves are slower and repeat; "Half speed" slows everything.
  • The page: moves and camera on top, everything else folded under "More".

Her own first moves

  • Written for her as the quick, sporty one. The Jester's set was the reference for what a set should hold, not copied.
  • Ready: she breathes, shifts her weight, and every few seconds bounces twice on her toes. She blinks.
  • Walk: rebuilt. She lands on her heel and pushes off her toes, her knee gives as each foot lands, her hips turn and dip with each stride, her shoulders turn against her hips and her head stays on where she is going.
  • Jog: the same stride made quick: leaning in, heels kicking up, elbows bent and pumping.
  • Talk: her own. Weight on one hip, one quick hand at chest height, small nods, her mouth moving through her painted mouths.
  • Moonsteel Draw: the bow from her back, the full draw, the arrow loosed, the bow put away. It replaces "Take aim".
  • Sword: drawn from her left hip, a guard, one full cut with a long step, and away.
  • Knives: both from the back of her belt, the first thrown with her right hand and the second with her left.
  • Cheer: a dip, a jump with one arm thrown up, a soft landing, a smile. Limber up: bouncing on her toes, rolling her shoulders, shaking out her arms.
  • Her bags and charms move with all of it.

Her hair and face

  • The younger face, with her hair left grey, as you chose. The top of her head slopes back from the band and the bun sits on the back of her head.

The faces

  • "Try a face" swaps the face painted on her head. Her head shape, hair, band and glasses do not change. Blink, talk and smile work on all three.
  • Younger is her own painted face with the lines of age taken out: forehead lines, crow's feet, the shade under her eyes and the folds beside her mouth are lifted toward the skin round them. Eyes, brows, lashes, lips and her mole are kept. Her hair is still grey.
  • The Fox's is the Fox's painted face laid on the same head: hazel eyes, freckles, red lips. Her skin is toned to the Ranger's so face and neck agree, and her eyes are moved 3 mm apart to sit in the Ranger's eye sockets.
  • Rough: only the front of the face changes. The sides of her cheeks and her ears keep the paint of her own face, so a seam of tone can show at three-quarter.

  • The strap hooks now move with her skin, so the outer curls ride her shoulder when she lifts her arms.
  • The straps are redrawn cleanly over the top of each shoulder, front to back, where they used to blur.

Her belts, bags and strap hooks

  • Two studded belts, each a real loop tilted round her hips, crossing front and back. The ring buckle is at her front right; the square buckle and blue keeper at her front left. 2.4 cm wide, with tucked ends and punched holes.
  • The satchel hangs by two loops on her right hip, built to its own painting. 20 cm across, 19 tall. The pouch hangs behind her left hip. 9 cm across, 14 tall.
  • Four strap hooks, front and back: a brass buckle with a black iron curl either side.
  • A choice I made: your paintings draw each bag face-on in every view, which one bag cannot do. The satchel hangs on the side of her right hip turned a little forward, so it shows from the front, the side and the back. Say if you would rather have it further round to the front.
