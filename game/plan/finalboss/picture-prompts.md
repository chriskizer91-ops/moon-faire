# Moon Faire: pictures for the final boss

Made on November 15, 2026 by the "Levelling" session, for task board F11. These are model sheets: the thing alone, seen
from three sides, for building it in code. **Every name is a stand-in.** No witches and no people in any of them.

Chris, November 15, 1:58 pm: the first rough model "is a good first draft of phase one of the boss and it should lose hp
but not take visible damage and then at zero hp transform into its final form which looks broken larger and more
vibrant". So there are two forms, and F1 and F2 are the two pictures to make. F3 is one more if he wants it.

Make F1 first. If the picture maker takes a picture to work from, give it F1's picture (or a screenshot of the model
page) along with prompt F2, so the final form is plainly the same thing.

Both forms are made of the same parts: a sphere, flat blades, rings, an iron bowl, two hands, four lanterns. The final
form is those parts pulled apart and lit up, so it costs about the same to draw as the first.

## F1  Form one: the fire, whole

Shape: 3:2, 1536 x 1024 or larger.  Save as: `final-fire-form1-sheet.png`.

```
Model sheet of a single towering fantasy boss for my role-playing game, Moon Faire, full figure, shown three times side by side on one sheet: front view, three-quarter view, back view. Each view is the same size and stands on the same ground line.

WHAT IT IS. An ancient beacon fire that would not go out, grown into a black sun. This is its first form: whole, calm and perfectly kept, with no crack, chip or wound anywhere on it. A floating black sphere like an eclipse, about a quarter of the figure's height, perfectly round and smooth, with a thin glowing rose-white rim. Around the sphere, an even ring of thirty or so long flat flame-shaped blades, all the same length, glowing deep crimson at the root and pale rose-white at the tips, like a corona. Three unbroken engraved old-gold iron rings orbit the sphere at different tilts, like the rings of an old astronomer's globe. A curtain of twelve long ash-rose streamers hangs straight down from under the sphere into a low, wide, three-legged black iron brazier bowl on the ground. The bowl's rim is set with short iron thorns, and a dull crimson glow lies inside it. To either side of the streamers float two large open hands made of overlapping dark iron blades, with no arms, palms turned inward as if tending the fire. Four small old glass-and-iron fair lanterns, each with a small warm amber flame inside, hang on short chains from four hooked iron arms on the bowl's rim.

SIZE. The whole thing is about eight metres tall and five wide; the brazier bowl would come up to a person's chest.

COLOURS. Eclipse black, old gold iron, deep crimson, pale rose-white, ash rose. The only warm amber is the four small lanterns. Nothing green and nothing blue.

STYLE. A realistic, richly painted picture with believable metal, cloth and fire, in a dark, beautiful fairy-tale mood. Elegant and solemn, not horror: no gore, no face, no eyes. Bold simple shapes with a strong silhouette and strong contrast of light and dark, and little fine surface texture.

BACKDROP AND LIGHT. A flat dark plum-grey backdrop, colour #383043, with no scenery, no floor pattern and no cast shadows on the backdrop. Even, soft light, so every part can be seen; the glow is its own.

LEAVE OUT. People, hands of people, creatures, lettering, labels, numbers, arrows, borders and frames.
```

## F2  The final form: the fire, broken open

Shape: 3:2, 1536 x 1024 or larger.  Save as: `final-fire-form2-sheet.png`.

```
Model sheet of a single towering fantasy boss for my role-playing game, Moon Faire, full figure, shown three times side by side on one sheet: front view, three-quarter view, back view. Each view is the same size and stands on the same ground line.

WHAT IT IS. An ancient beacon fire that would not go out, in its final form. It was a calm black sun with a corona of crimson blades, three gold rings, two iron hands and an iron brazier bowl. It has been ended once and would not end, and it has broken open. It is larger, wilder and far brighter than before, and everything that was whole is now in pieces held in the air around a blazing heart. The black eclipse sphere has burst into seven large curved shards of smooth black shell, hanging apart like the pieces of a broken egg with wide gaps between them. In the gaps blazes the heart: a ball of white-hot fire, pure white at the centre, hot rose around it, fierce crimson at its edge, far bigger and brighter than the thin rim of light it used to have. At the very centre of the white, one tiny ordinary candle flame of warm amber. The corona is uneven now: forty or so flat flame-shaped blades of very different lengths, the longest twice as long as the shortest, a few snapped off and drifting free, hot crimson at the root and white at the tips. The three old-gold iron rings are broken into six curved pieces of different lengths, turning at wild tilts, their snapped ends glowing white-hot rose. The streamers have caught fire: twelve ribbons of crimson flame whipping outward and upward instead of hanging down. The three-legged black iron brazier bowl is split into two halves, tipped apart and lifted a little off the ground, with crimson light pouring up between them. One of the two great iron hands has come apart into its separate dark iron blades, drifting loose; the other hand is whole and held close under the heart, as if still trying to hold it together. Four small old fair lanterns hang open and dark on their chains from the bowl's halves, their glass broken and no flame in them.

SIZE. About twelve metres tall and nine wide: half as tall again as it was. The split brazier bowl is still only chest height to a person, so the growth shows.

COLOURS. Eclipse black, old gold iron, hot crimson, bright rose, and pure white at the heart. Much more saturated and luminous than a calm fire: it should be the brightest thing in the game. The only warm amber is the one tiny flame at the very centre. Nothing green and nothing blue.

STYLE. A realistic, richly painted picture with believable metal and fire, in a dark, beautiful fairy-tale mood. Terrible and beautiful, not horror: no gore, no face, no eyes. Bold simple shapes with a strong silhouette and strong contrast of light and dark, and little fine surface texture. Every piece is a clean, separate shape with clear air round it.

BACKDROP AND LIGHT. A flat dark plum-grey backdrop, colour #383043, with no scenery, no floor pattern and no cast shadows on the backdrop. Even, soft light, so every part can be seen; the glow is its own.

LEAVE OUT. People, hands of people, creatures, lettering, labels, numbers, arrows, borders and frames.
```
