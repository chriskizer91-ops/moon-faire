The final boss in two forms

You said the rough model is a good first draft of form one, that it should lose Life without showing damage, and that at zero it changes into a final form that is broken, larger and more vibrant. You asked for picture prompts for both forms, and whether the fight should be one body alone, harder than the gate fights, or the great wraith's numbers under new names with the same helpers. I played both with the eight testers.

My answer: use both of your ideas, one after the other

  • Form one is your second idea. The fire, whole, with the great wraith's own numbers under new names and the same four helpers.
  • The final form is your first idea. The fire, broken open, alone, with a quarter of the Life and the same blows, acting twice as often.
  • On hard, only an expert wins: 13% of the time with no finds and 63% with all 18. Today's last fight gives her 26% and 80%, so this is one notch harder at every setting.
  • It needs no new rule beyond "the second form starts with the HP and MP the party has left".

It also plays the story out in the rules: the Jester gives the fire its ending, and it refuses.

What the testers showed

In every table the top number is how often that player wins with no finds, and the number under it is with all 18. The party is level 20 and arrives fresh from a rest spot.

1. The helpers are most of what makes the great wraith hard

Take the helpers away and leave the great wraith its own numbers, and an attentive player wins 100% on easy and 100% on medium. With the helpers she wins 80% and 3.1%. So one body alone has to be far stronger than the great wraith before it is harder than the gate fights.

2. One body alone can be made hard enough, but it is a narrower fight

I gave one body 30% more Life than the great wraith in form one, then a final form with 40% of the great wraith's Life that hits 10% harder. That lands hard in the right place (the expert wins 17%, or 51% with every find). Three things came with it:

  • Easy is easier than the gate before it. An attentive player wins 100% on easy, where she wins about 80% against the great bramble.
  • Part of the party's kit has nothing to do. With one foe the Fox can never cast Crazy Quilt, Pins and Needles has nothing to spread to, Sealed Fate's splash hits nobody, and there is never a choice of whom to hit.
  • It swings on one trick. My testers spend their MP in form one as if it were the whole fight. An expert who knows the second form is coming and holds MP back wins 76% on hard with no finds, up from 17%. So it would be very hard the first time and much easier every time after.

The fight I would build

Numbers are on the fight simulator's scale, where the Jester has 1,000 HP. Names of blows are yours to choose.

Form one: the fire, whole

  • Life: 8,000, 9,600, 11,520, 13,330 on easy, medium, hard and crazy. The great wraith's own.
  • Blows: the great wraith's three, renamed. A plain blow of 130, a marked blow of 440 at one witch, and a blow on everyone of 110 that grows by 25 each time. Each is 20% bigger for every level above 20.
  • Helpers: the same four the great wraith has, two of the Shadow Wraith's size and two of the wisp's size.
  • It shows no damage, however low its Life goes. Only the Life bar tells.
  • It ends as every boss ends now: its Life runs out or the Jester seals it, the Curtain Call falls, and anyone who fainted gets up for the bow with a quarter of her HP.

The final form: the fire, broken open

  • The curtain tears open and it is back, alone, larger and brighter.
  • Life: 2,000, 2,400, 2,880, 3,330. A quarter of form one's.
  • Blows: the same three at the same sizes, but it acts twice as often. That is already the rule for a boss left alone.
  • The party gets nothing back. It fights on with the HP and MP it has, Io's Deity does not return, and the fire starts with no Fate on it.
  • This one ends for good, in the real Curtain Call.

Herbs do not rescue it

I gave the party 20 HP herbs and 20 MP herbs for the fight, then 60 of each. Nobody did better. Eating a herb costs a turn, and the way my testers use them they did slightly worse (the expert on hard: 2.5% and 29%). So a very big bag does not break the last fight. A cleverer herb habit might gain a little; I have not built one.

WHAT IT IS. An ancient beacon fire that would not go out, grown into a black sun. This is its first form: whole, calm and perfectly kept, with no crack, chip or wound anywhere on it. A floating black sphere like an eclipse, about a quarter of the figure's height, perfectly round and smooth, with a thin glowing rose-white rim. Around the sphere, an even ring of thirty or so long flat flame-shaped blades, all the same length, glowing deep crimson at the root and pale rose-white at the tips, like a corona. Three unbroken engraved old-gold iron rings orbit the sphere at different tilts, like the rings of an old astronomer's globe. A curtain of twelve long ash-rose streamers hangs straight down from under the sphere into a low, wide, three-legged black iron brazier bowl on the ground. The bowl's rim is set with short iron thorns, and a dull crimson glow lies inside it. To either side of the streamers float two large open hands made of overlapping dark iron blades, with no arms, palms turned inward as if tending the fire. Four small old glass-and-iron fair lanterns, each with a small warm amber flame inside, hang on short chains from four hooked iron arms on the bowl's rim.

SIZE. The whole thing is about eight metres tall and five wide; the brazier bowl would come up to a person's chest.

COLOURS. Eclipse black, old gold iron, deep crimson, pale rose-white, ash rose. The only warm amber is the four small lanterns. Nothing green and nothing blue.

STYLE. A realistic, richly painted picture with believable metal, cloth and fire, in a dark, beautiful fairy-tale mood. Elegant and solemn, not horror: no gore, no face, no eyes. Bold simple shapes with a strong silhouette and strong contrast of light and dark, and little fine surface texture.

BACKDROP AND LIGHT. A flat dark plum-grey backdrop, colour #383043, with no scenery, no floor pattern and no cast shadows on the backdrop. Even, soft light, so every part can be seen; the glow is its own.

Three things for you to say

  1. Which fight? Helpers first and then alone (my pick), one body alone throughout, or the great wraith's fight in one form.
  2. When form one ends, do the fallen get up? As measured they do, at a quarter HP, because that is what the Curtain Call does now. If they stay down, the expert wins hard 3.4% of the time, or 37% with every find.
  3. Who are the helpers in form one? Wraiths and brambles it has called, or the four lanterns themselves. The numbers are the same either way.

What these numbers are: 2,000 fights for every cell of the big tables and 1,000 for the rest, played by the eight pretend players through the approved fight rules, which are not changed. The helpers use the wraiths' numbers until the bramble foes have their own, and the finds are still to be tuned again for fights that carry HP and MP over.
