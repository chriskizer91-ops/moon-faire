Levelling for the bigger game

You asked for 5 to 10 times the fights of the first plan, and a wider span of levels, now that there is open land to run off into. Here are three sizes of game. Each was played through 2,000 times for each play style, with the combat engine you approved deciding every fight. Nothing inside a fight changes.

Chosen: option B

20 levels, 180 fights to finish, full-size fights, and level 20 is the top. Chris, November 14, 2026, 7:30 pm.

It is written into the plan as game/plan/levelling/plan-chosen.json, and played again from that file alone: 180 wins to finish, 3 to 6 hours of fights.

What the top level changes. The party stops at level 20, so fighting more than you must no longer makes the last gate easier: it stays at 81% for a player who pays attention. The things she finds are the way to ease it, as you described for the first plan.

The first plan, for comparison: 5 levels, about 24 fights, 25 to 45 minutes of fights.

All three climb at nearly the same pace. A longer game is the same climb carried further.

The same in all three

  • XP for a foe is unchanged. A level-1 wisp gives 10, a Shadow Wraith 40, a great wraith 400, and 20% more for every foe level. The three set fights at the fair still take the party to level 2.
  • The land beyond the fair is in rings. Each ring is worth three levels. Its foes run from the level the party arrives at to one above the level it leaves at. So walking foes are between 2 levels under the party and 4 over, which is your rule for ordinary foes.
  • A gate fight ends each ring, and it opens at the level the ring ends on, as the lamp room door opens at level 5 today. The last gate is the big one from the first plan: the great wraith, two Shadow Wraiths and two wisps, at the top level.
  • Levels come quickly, then slowly: a level every 5 or 6 wins at the start, every 13 or 14 near the end.
  • Walking fights stay safe. A player who pays attention won every walking fight in every run, even against foes 4 levels over. A player pressing any button lost 2, 2, 3 walking fights in a whole game of A, B, C.
  • The last gate is as hard as you approved. At the party's level it is won 6% / 81% / 99% of the time (any button / paying attention / expert). One level over, 61% / 100% / 100%.
  • Fighting more than you must is the easy setting. A player who fights half as many fights again in every ring reaches the last gate about one level ahead (A: 156 wins, B: 227 wins, C: 295 wins). That turns the last gate from 81% to 100% for an attentive player, with no change to the rules. These runs had no top level. The chosen plan stops at level 20, so this does not apply to it.

What these numbers do not cover

  • The hours are a guess. They assume 4 to 9 seconds for a witch's turn, as the first plan did. Nobody has timed a real fight with its animations yet, and the long moves run 13 to 15 seconds at normal speed.
  • Every fight starts fresh, with full HP and MP. The fight piece can carry HP and MP from one fight to the next. If you want that, a long stretch between rest spots gets harder, and it needs its own measuring.
  • Only three kinds of foe exist. New kinds for the moor will change what a walking fight is worth and how long it runs.
  • Walking and talking time is not in the hours. Nor is how often a fight comes while she walks. The open world has to offer about this many fights on the way through each ring.

What I made up

  • Three levels to a ring, and 4, 6 or 8 rings.
  • Which line-ups are met in which ring, and the gate fights other than the first plan's own.
  • The XP totals for each level. They are sized so the climb takes the fights you asked for.
