# Moon Faire: what is settled, for whoever maps the demo

Written November 13, 2026 from Chris's lore notes (project docs `lore/README.md`, `lore/discussion-notes.md`) and the approved starter-world plan (`claude/starter-world-step1-whole-game-plan.md`). Chris's words win over anything here. Names of people and places are placeholders; the lore is Chris's.

## The world
- The world is Thareia; its moon is Auros. This story is on a different continent from Chris's other games, in a different time. The continent has no name yet.
- A Dark Ages fantasy that runs on solar crystal power that looks like steam. Airships are a big part of the world.
- Auros is almost locked in place over Thareia and goes round once every 500 years. It hangs nearly still in the sky. Any one place sees it for about 250 years.
- The Moon Faire is an ordinary travelling fair that follows the moon round the planet: the greatest melting pot of cultures, identities and knowledge there is. For most places its coming is a once-in-a-lifetime thing.
- Moon phases are NOT part of anyone's magic.
- The moon is where the wealthy live; other planets are reached by space airship. None of that needs to be in this demo.

## The party: four witches who travel with the fair
All four are together from the start, and their story together goes back before the game. They have no names yet: call them the Jester, the Fox, Io and the Ranger.
- The Jester: the strongest. Witty and cunning; she laughs, dances and taunts so foes strike at her, and shrugs the blows off. Her great spell ends fights. Her lesson is to defend so her friends survive.
- The Fox: a playful fabric artist of chaos magic. Scissors, needle, thread and a bell; she sews and cuts the fabric of reality. Cold blue foxfire. Costume fox ears. She confuses foes and gets the party away.
- Io, the Herbal Witch (red hat): the healer; herbs and potions; summons with a paladin's seriousness.
- The Ranger: a princess with a little magic; best with bow, sword and knives.

## The airship captain
Someone the four meet, not a playable character. A grey floppy witch hat, black glasses, long dark hair and a beard, a ruffled cream shirt, a black corset, red-and-black trousers, platform boots. No name yet. His 3D model is being built in another session. In the plan the airship is his.

## The demo, as approved (ten areas)
One evening that darkens as they go: dusk at the fair, night on the moor, deep night at the beacon. Auros is in every sky, upper left, always in the same spot.

Story, six beats (a suggestion Chris approved as a placeholder): the four are at the fair at dusk; wisps carry off the twelve lamps of the captain's airship; the four win them back on the moor; a Shadow Wraith bars the road at the standing stones; with all twelve lit the captain flies them to a dead airship beacon on a crag; a great wraith at the top hoards the stolen light.

Zone 1, the Moon Faire (party level 1 to 2):
- W1 the fair square: the start. Chris has painted it: a cobbled square at dusk with a well, a cloth-seller's awning, a striped round tent, stalls, lamp posts and bunting.
- W2 the stall row and the sparring ring.
- W3 the mooring field: the captain and his airship. The twelve lamps light up on the moored ship as they are won back.
- Three set fights as wisps come into the fair (one in each of W1, W2, W3) and practice bouts in the ring. No walking fights.

Zone 2, the wilds (party level 2 to 4): walking fights about every 14 metres, one set fight in each of W4, W5, W6.
- W4 the Last Lantern (holds 3 lamps).
- W5 the standing stones (4 lamps): a Shadow Wraith bars the road.
- W6 the still pool and the broken arch (5 lamps): a camp, a rest spot.
- W7 the hollow: hidden, off the path. A find: a healing amulet.

Zone 3, the Dark Beacon (party level 4 to 5):
- W8 the landing ledge: the captain and the airship again.
- W9 the beacon stair: walking fights; a find off the path (a charm).
- F5 the lamp room: the great wraith, two Shadow Wraiths and two wisps. Its door opens at level 5.

The gate: zone 3 opens when all twelve lamps are back. A lamp only comes from winning a fight in the wilds.

Rules of the game that matter for what can happen:
- Foes are the wraith family only: the wisp, the Shadow Wraith, the great wraith. A lamp-carrying wisp is the same wisp.
- Every fight starts with full HP and MP. Losing costs nothing: the party wakes at the last rest spot.
- One witch walks the scene; nobody follows her. Talking shows a picture card beside a text box.
- The only other people in the game are people the party talks to. There is no background crowd.
- Combat is turn-based, "like chess". Easier or harder comes from simple finds, never from changing the rules.

## The fair folk (people the party can talk to)
Eleven painted people exist as 3D models, all stand-ins Chris may rename or re-cast. Each can stand, turn to face the party, talk, play emotes (nod, shake head, wave, greet bow, shrug, laugh, point, think, surprised, sad, cross, beckon, refuse, thanks), walk or run to a spot, and sit on a seat.
- `old-lamp-seller`: An old woman who has sold oil lamps and wicks at the fair for forty years. Hello: beckon. Talks: calm. Most like them: nod, beckon, think.
- `pie-seller`: A sturdy middle-aged woman who sells hot meat pies from a cart by the gate. Hello: wave. Talks: lively, calm. Most like them: beckon, laugh, point.
- `ballad-singer`: A young woman who sings old songs on a small stage between the stalls each night. Hello: greet_bow. Talks: calm, lively. Most like them: greet_bow, think, sad.
- `fortune-teller`: A tall, thin fortune-teller who reads palms and cards in a dim tent at the edge of the fair. Hello: beckon. Talks: calm. Most like them: think, beckon, point.
- `old-lamplighter`: An elderly man who walks the fair at dusk, lighting and trimming the lanterns along the stalls. Hello: wave. Talks: calm. Most like them: point, nod, wave.
- `old-night-watchman`: An old night-watchman who walks the fair rows after dark, checking stall shutters and lamps. Hello: nod. Talks: calm, gruff. Most like them: point, nod, cross.
- `chestnut-roaster`: A cheerful middle-aged man who roasts chestnuts over a brazier at the fair gate. Hello: beckon. Talks: lively. Most like them: laugh, beckon, wave.
- `fair-baker`: A stout, cheerful baker who sells warm loaves and buns from his oven cart at the night fair. Hello: wave. Talks: lively, calm. Most like them: laugh, thanks, beckon.
- `ribbon-dyer`: An old, quiet dyer who boils and sells dyed ribbons and cloth lengths from a steaming stall at the fair. Hello: greet_bow. Talks: calm. Most like them: think, shrug, nod.
- `farm-lad`: A cheerful young farmhand who carts hay and feed for the fair's ponies and stays on at night to help. Hello: wave. Talks: lively. Most like them: wave, laugh, surprised.
- `gate-keeper`: A stern middle-aged woman who takes the coins at the fair gate and watches who comes and goes after dark. Hello: beckon. Talks: gruff. Most like them: cross, refuse, point.
