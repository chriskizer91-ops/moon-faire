People from a template

A test for Moon Faire, November 13. The Jester's finished model is the template. A Claude model reads one line about a person and writes a small file: colours, skin, a repainted face, hair, a hat, simple clothes. The Jester's skeleton and all her moves are reused, so every person can already stand, turn and walk.

    What this says
    • With the template already built, a new background person costs between half a cent and about 18 cents, and takes under a minute.
    • The cheapest model makes a person you can recognise from the description. The two dearer ones make cleaner clothes and fix more of their own mistakes after seeing the picture.
    • Each person is about 12 thousand triangles and 15 draws. The Jester took 106 thousand when she walked the fair square on your laptop, so a person is about a ninth of her. No Claude session can measure speed on your laptop, so how many people a scene can hold still needs a test there.
    • What the template cannot do yet: every person has the Jester's body and the shape of her face, so men and stout people only look right from a distance and under a coat or apron. That is a limit of the template, not of the model writing the file, and a second template body would lift it.
